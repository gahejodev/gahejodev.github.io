Complete preparation syllabus

The first five pedagogy domains below follow the official 2024 Elementary syllabus themes. Expanded subtopics are a study guide, not a claim that every item is a separately prescribed exam question.

1. Methods of Teaching & Foundations of Education
  • Instructional approaches: teacher-centred, learner-centred, activity-based, inquiry-based, problem-based and experiential learning.
  • Effective questioning: open/closed questions, probing, wait-time, higher-order questions and checking understanding.
  • Cooperative learning: group roles, peer tutoring, think-pair-share, jigsaw and collaborative tasks.
  • Bloom’s Taxonomy: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse, Evaluate, Create; write measurable learning outcomes.
  • Lesson planning: objectives, prior knowledge, introduction, teaching-learning activities, resources, differentiation, assessment, closure and reflection.
  • Foundations: ideological, philosophical, sociological, psychological and historical foundations of education.
  • National Professional Standards for Teachers in Pakistan: subject knowledge, human growth, instructional planning/strategies, assessment, learning environment, communication, collaboration, professional development and ethics.
2. Child Development & Educational Psychology
  • Domains of development: physical, cognitive, language, social, emotional and moral.
  • Developmental principles: continuity, sequence, individual pace, heredity-environment interaction and readiness.
  • Piaget: cognitive development and stages; Vygotsky: ZPD and scaffolding; Erikson: psychosocial development; Kohlberg: moral development; Bruner: discovery learning; Bandura: observational learning.
  • Learning theories: behaviourism (Pavlov, Skinner, reinforcement), cognitivism, constructivism (Piaget/Vygotsky) and humanism (Maslow/Rogers).
  • Motivation: intrinsic/extrinsic, needs, goals, reinforcement, self-efficacy and supportive feedback.
  • Individual differences, prior knowledge, language, disability, giftedness, socio-economic context and special educational needs.
  • Age-appropriate strategies, inclusive practices, Universal Design for Learning (UDL) and referral/support pathways.
3. Classroom Management
  • Positive classroom climate, routines, rules, expectations and time management.
  • Physical environment: seating, visibility, ventilation, learning corners, displays and safe movement.
  • Social environment: respect, belonging, shared values, cultural responsiveness and student voice.
  • Preventive management, positive reinforcement, restorative responses, conflict resolution and behaviour support.
  • Managing large and multi-grade classes; low-cost teaching aids and peer learning.
  • Classroom observation, anecdotal records, attendance/participation data and reflective practice.
  • Differentiated learning: adapt content, process, product and learning environment; flexible grouping.
4. Classroom Assessment, Test Development & Evaluation
  • Assessment, measurement, testing and evaluation: definitions and differences.
  • Diagnostic, formative and summative assessment; assessment for/as/of learning.
  • Feedback: timely, specific, actionable, respectful and linked to learning goals.
  • Assessment-embedded lesson plans, exit tickets, observation, oral questioning and peer/self-assessment.
  • MCQs, short answers, constructed responses, essays, performance tasks, projects and portfolios.
  • Rubrics, criteria, marking schemes, test blueprints/table of specifications and item analysis.
  • Validity, reliability, fairness, objectivity, practicality and accessibility.
  • Teacher-made tests, standards, learning domains and Bloom-aligned questions.
5. School, Community & Teacher
  • School and community structures/functions in Pakistan; school as a social institution.
  • Education and social development; family, religious institutions and other social institutions.
  • Culture, cultural elements, language diversity, inclusion and cultural competency.
  • School-community partnership, parent engagement, SMC/community participation and communication.
  • Working context of Pakistani teachers, professional identity, collaboration and ethical responsibilities.
  • Confidentiality, child protection, non-discrimination, professional boundaries and responsible use of student data.
6. Pedagogical Content Knowledge (PCK) — subject teaching
  • Mathematics: number sense, place value, operations, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio, geometry, measurement, data handling; diagnose misconceptions and use concrete → pictorial → abstract progression.
  • Science: living things, plants, human body, matter, force, energy, environment, earth/space; inquiry, observation, fair tests, prediction and evidence-based explanation.
  • Social Studies: geography, history, civics, citizenship, maps, local community, culture, rights/responsibilities; use timelines, maps, discussion and local examples.
  • English: listening, speaking, reading, writing, vocabulary, grammar, comprehension and phonics; scaffold language and integrate the four skills.
  • Urdu/Sindhi: reading fluency, comprehension, vocabulary, grammar, writing, oral expression and mother-tongue support.
  • For every subject, practise: identify misconception → choose teaching strategy → plan activity → check learning → give feedback.
7. Psychometric Assessment — preparation guidance
  • Practise careful reading, consistency, situational judgement and professional decision-making.
  • Choose responses that prioritise student safety, fairness, inclusion, honesty, confidentiality and evidence-based action.
  • Do not try to guess a hidden personality profile or fake a preferred persona. Answer honestly and consistently.
  • STEDA's public FAQ assigns 20% to psychometric assessment; the exact Phase-II instrument and scoring details must be confirmed from the official test notice/RFP.
8. Content Knowledge — school subjects
  • Revise core concepts from the relevant Sindh/DCAR school curriculum for the licence category and level announced.
  • Mathematics: arithmetic, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio, geometry, measurement, patterns and basic statistics.
  • Science: biology, physical science, earth/environmental science, scientific method and everyday applications.
  • Social Studies: Pakistan/Sindh geography and history, citizenship, society, culture and map skills.
  • English and mother tongue: grammar, vocabulary, comprehension, sentence construction and language use.
  • Use the prescribed textbook/curriculum, not random advanced material. The published 2024 Elementary syllabus specifically names Classes 1–8.
Do not confuse two published formats: STEDA’s FAQ states a 100-mark framework of 30/20/50. The older IBA model paper shows a 300-mark practice structure: one case-study ERQ (100), ten CRQs (150 total) and 50 MCQs (50 total), with 25/50/25 weightage. Use the latter to practise written reasoning, not as a confirmed Phase-II paper blueprint.

Case-study & constructed-response practice

Practise structured answers for realistic classroom situations. These are original practice prompts, not official STEDA questions.

Answer framework: Identify the issue → use evidence from the scenario → choose an inclusive response → explain how you will assess impact → plan follow-up.
Case 1 • Mixed ability

One lesson, different learning levels

In a Grade 5 class of 48 learners, some can solve two-digit multiplication while others still struggle with place value. There are few textbooks. Explain how you would teach the same objective while supporting both groups.

View answer outline
Start with a diagnostic task. Model with bundles, drawings and place-value columns. Use flexible groups: guided concrete practice for learners needing support and extension problems for learners ready to progress. Check every learner with an exit ticket, analyse errors and regroup next lesson.
Case 2 • Inclusion

Quiet learner with a language barrier

A learner recently joined your class and rarely speaks in front of peers. Other students laugh at the learner’s pronunciation. What steps will you take?

View answer outline
Stop ridicule and establish respectful expectations. Speak privately to understand the learner’s strengths and needs. Use visuals, key vocabulary, pair rehearsal and multiple ways to demonstrate understanding. Value the home language, monitor wellbeing and review participation without publicly labelling the learner.
Case 3 • Assessment

Half the class has the same misconception

An exit ticket shows that 22 of 40 learners believe 1/4 is greater than 1/3 because four is greater than three. How will you respond next lesson?

View answer outline
Treat the results as evidence of a shared misconception. Revisit unit fractions with equal-sized fraction strips, paper folding and a number line. Ask learners to predict and justify. Use hinge questions and a new exit ticket to check progress, then provide a guided group for learners who still need support.
Case 4 • Safeguarding

A learner reports bullying

A student privately reports repeated threats from classmates and asks you not to tell anyone. Describe immediate and follow-up actions.

View answer outline
Listen calmly and do not promise absolute secrecy. Check immediate safety, record the concern factually and report through the school’s designated safeguarding procedure. Maintain confidentiality, avoid public confrontation, arrange appropriate support and follow up on safety.
Case 5 • Science inquiry

Low-cost practical learning

You have no laboratory equipment but need to teach evaporation and condensation. Plan a safe, low-cost activity and explain how you will assess learning.

View answer outline
Use a teacher-supervised demonstration with a small amount of warm water and a cool lid to observe droplets. Explain safety boundaries and do not let learners handle hot water. Ask learners to predict, observe, record and explain. Assess with labelled diagrams, oral explanations and an exit question.

Roman Urdu quick revision

Pedagogy: Sirf lecture dena teaching nahi. Students ko activity, sawal, discussion aur practice ke zariye seekhne ka mauqa dein.

Formative assessment: Lesson ke dauran check karein ke bachay samajh rahe hain ya nahi; result ke mutabiq apni teaching badlein.

Differentiation: Learning objective aik ho sakta hai, lekin support aur activity students ki zaroorat ke mutabiq mukhtalif ho sakti hai.

Quick revision notes

Bloom’s Taxonomy

Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → Create. Use observable verbs: list, explain, solve, compare, justify, design.

Assessment types

Diagnostic: before teaching. Formative: during learning to improve it. Summative: at the end to judge achievement.

Constructivism

Learners actively build understanding using prior knowledge, experience, discussion and reflection. Teacher facilitates and scaffolds.

Scaffolding & ZPD

Vygotsky’s Zone of Proximal Development is the space between independent performance and what a learner can do with guidance. Gradually remove support.

Validity & reliability

Validity: does the assessment measure what it intends to measure? Reliability: are results consistent? Fairness and accessibility also matter.

Differentiated instruction

Adjust content, process, product or environment to learner readiness, interests and needs while keeping meaningful learning goals.

Cooperative learning

Structured small-group learning with positive interdependence, individual accountability, interaction and group reflection.

Effective feedback

Specific, timely and actionable: explain what was done well, what needs improvement and the next step.
